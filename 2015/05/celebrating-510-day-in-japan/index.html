Celebrating 510 Day In Japan

May 13, 2015 Mike Garrett
Long Live The 510

What do you do on the day immediately following one of the greatest car meets ever? You go to another car meet. This past Sunday, less than 12 hours after we finished up our cruise night in Minato Mirai 21 I found myself in Yokohama once more for another gathering.

The date was May 10th, and that meant it was time to celebrate 510 Day – Datsun 510 that is. Sure, I was still exhausted from the previous night’s action, but that wasn’t going to stop me from spending an afternoon celebrating this iconic Nissan model.

I was actually told about this meet by my friend Tamura-san, a 510 owner who created the event so that Kanto area 510 faithful could get-together to celebrate their favorite car.

And he picked a damn cool place to throw a car meet! The parking lot at the Yokohama Port Symbol Tower is a spot well known for its easy-going vibe and fantastic views of Tokyo Bay.

The weather on 510 Day could not have been better – sunny and warm with a pleasant breeze blowing in off the bay. In other words, perfect conditions for a car meet.

With about 40 cars in attendance this wasn’t a gigantic event, but after the craziness of Saturday I was perfectly okay with that. It was a great way to unwind after a long night.

The Datsun 510/Nissan Bluebird occupies an interesting place in Japan’s classic car scene. It might not share quite the same legendary reputation of the Skyline GT-R or Fairlady Z, but 510 owners are some of the coolest and most passionate people you’ll find.

When it comes to customization the 510 is a versatile machine, and its owners come from a wide range of backgrounds. Some are restorers, some are weekend racers, and some just like to cruise.

One Bluebird, Many Styles

The 510 was immensely popular overseas, and it’s quite common to see 510s in Japan that have been modified in a distinctively American style. We’re talking stuff like door mirrors, fender flares, Libre wheels and BRE chin spoilers.

This sedan is a perfect example of an a vintage American-style 510: BRE spoiler, polished slot mags and Cooper white letter tires. It’s USDM style from a time before it was even a thing.

This sedan on the other hand was modified in a more homegrown Japanese way. Check out the RS Watanabe wheels with Advan Neova rubber, fender mirrors and the air intake in the headlight hole. I think I love both styles in equal amounts.

Another cool thing about the 510 is its wide variety of body styles. All of the different shapes were present and accounted for in Yokohama on Sunday.

There’s the two-door sedan – a body type that’s especially popular among those going for a US-inspired look.

Then there’s the four-door sedan, which seems to be the most popular body style among Japanese-market Bluebirds.

Then there’s the station wagon. And what’s not to like about a 510 that has extra room for all your stuff?

Last but certainly not least is the two-door coupe with its unique roofline and straight-across tail lamps. Man, it’s hard to pick a favorite from this group!

The modifications made to the cars also spanned a wide spectrum, with some owners preferring period-correct setups with built L-motors and vintage wheels.

Others enjoy fitting their cars with modern parts, as exemplified by this four-door sitting on a set of 17-inch RAYS Volk Racing TE37s,

A couple of the cars were taken far beyond your typical resto-mod job. For example, Hosokawa-san’s fully-built 510 is packing turbo SR20 power and a bunch of trick suspension work.

You might recognize Hirano-san’s stunningly clean, naturally aspirated SR20 sedan from my recent Wekfest Japan coverage. Look out for full features on both this car and the turbo machine in the future.

The wide range of styles and approaches could be seen in the cars’ interiors as well. Small diameter racing wheel, stick shift and bucket seats…

Or a bench seat and a column shift automatic for some old fashioned cruisin’ style? Do it your way.

A Little Help From My Friends

While the meet was officially for 510s, there were plenty of other cool vintage Japanese machines that came to check out the festivities, including this rare Bluebird U Wagon lowered over a set of Drag Fortran wheels.

It might not be as recognizable as its older counterpart, but the 610 Bluebird Coupe is equally cool – especially when its dropped down on a set of Hayashi Streets like this one is.

The SoCal-inspired look of this Sunny Truck was a perfect fit with the ocean breeze blowing across the park. All it really needs is a couple of surfboards in the back!

I was quite pleased to see this C30 Laurel out there as well. The later C130 models always get a lot of love, but it’s not often you see a first generation car like this one.

Hey, who let the Toyota into the Nissan party? No complaints on my end, as this Tosco wheel equipped TE27 Levin was an absolute masterpiece.

I think it goes without saying that I felt an increasing urge to pick up a 510 after attending this meet. If only I didn’t already have two project cars waiting for me back in California…

The Yokohama 510 Day meet certainly wasn’t the largest event from my current Japan trip, but the beauty of this place is that even the casual gatherings are absolutely great.

Great cars, perfect weather and a beautiful setting – not a bad way to spend another lazy Sunday afternoon in Japan!
