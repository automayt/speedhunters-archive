Top Down And Backfiring: The Mclaren Experience

August 16, 2013 Dino Dalle Carbonare

Every time someone mentions top-down driving I have this image in my head of an autumn afternoon sitting in the cosseting confines of a luxurious and comfortable cabriolet, gently wafting down an open road headed towards a private weekend retreat. But the sort of top-down motoring I recently had the pleasure of experiencing in a candy red McLaren MP4-12C cabrio, couldn’t possibly be more different. All I remember from those six or so hours I spent with the car is deafening, face-rearranging wind, and a screaming V8 soundtrack punctuated by fluttering blow-off valves and the loudest backfires I’ve ever had the pleasure of causing.

I mean, if you get tossed the keys to one of the hottest supercars of the moment you might as well drive the wheels off it right? And that is exactly what I did. After picking the car up in central Tokyo I immediately jumped on the Shuto and pointed the car towards Shibuya, passing the fashionable district and continuing my journey towards the Tomei expressway. My destination was to be a favorite road of mine – one that I have been waiting to show you for a long time, but not until I had the right sort of car and just the right weather to get the best out of it. Today was that day.

The stretch of tarmac in question is called the Ashinoko Skyline. Not only is this the very first touge I ever drove in the Hakone area, but back in the day it was a popular road for car manufacturers to hold press events on. There are number of routes you can take to get there, but I ended up choosing the most fun, which included a very fast paced drive…

… on the Toyo Tires Turnpike – or the Hakone Turnpike as it’s always been more commonly known. Up until this point I had taken it pretty easy with the MP4-12C, slowly familiarising myself with the car and its simple yet intuitive controls. Compared to some of its direct competitors it does look quite sedate, but beneath the skin is an extremely advanced package.

The press car was pretty much fully loaded with most of the options ticked, except the carbon ceramic brake package. The brakes turned out to be the only uninspiring aspect of the car…

… which after a quick sprint up to the top of the Turnpike and then down through a series of challenging second gear corners, felt overworked and began fading and loosing their initial bite. It might be due to the fact that a lot of cars that are sold in Japan are fitted with less aggressive, and therefore quieter, brake pads, but that was the only instance I had the issue during the whole day. From the top of the mountain pass…

… it was a short 10-minute drive to the Ashinoko Skyline toll gate. Like most interesting roads around that area you have to pay for the beautiful views and billiard-table-smooth tarmac. But ¥600 (US$6) was a small price to pay for the enjoyment of putting this particular car through its paces.

So after passing the gate and paying up…

… the Dream Drive finally commenced.

This road brings back so many memories. I must have attended 20 or so press drives here in the past, and the very same stretch where I got to test the Nismo Z-tune back in 2005. The McLaren however, was unlike any other car I have ever driven here…

… that twin turbo V8 delivering one of the most memorable experiences I’ve ever had. Even at idle you can feel this isn’t your regular high-performance engine. It has a little lumpiness, in the way a highly-tuned race motor does. McLaren purchased the rights to the Tom Walkinshaw Racing version of the Nissan VRH35 – the engine that powered the R390 GT1 at LeMans in 1998, but in this ‘M838T’ guise, what McLaren has created is very much a new concept.

The somewhat small 3.8L capacity for a V8 is what gives it its character. Despite having a decent amount of torque from as low as 2,000rpm it just entices you to rev it more, and that’s when it rewards. From 4,000rpm onwards all of its fury is on-tap.

I couldn’t help but draw similarities with the RB26DETT – an engine that feels very similar and loves to rev, but is not quite as explosive as this. So rev it I did…

… punching it all the way to the top of third gear when the road opened up a little…

… and then braking hard, shifting down to second…

… to be on-boost and using all of its 600hp when powering out. Since these pictures were shot at the same time that I was driving I did take it easy…

… cruising the whole Ashinoko Skyline till the very end, getting my images then putting the camera away and driving up and down the road a few more times at a more natural pace.

There are a great variety of corners to experiment with…

… and here the McLaren’s chassis and suspension tuning really came into its own. After picking the car up in Tokyo I instantly couldn’t believe how smoothly it glided over imperfections when the dampers were set in ‘N’ (Normal) mode. Rotate the Handling mode into ‘S’ (Sport) and the chassis instantly tightens up beneath you. The car becomes more alert and responsive to steering inputs, with almost no body roll to be felt. One click more and you are in ‘T’ (Track) mode, and it’s here that the car is in its most extreme setting. It’s perhaps too hard and edgy for normal roads, but it’s fun nonetheless.

I always make a point to stop during my drives. It’s good to rest and ponder over the car, admiring it and taking more pictures, and at the same time trying to make sense of what your body has just been put through. It had been about three hours and the MP4 had delivered one of the most intense driving experiences I’ve ever had.

At one point an old guy in his Land Rover stopped by for a look at the car and handed me a leaflet for an upcoming classic car meeting at Fuji Speedway, which was pretty cool. I guess he assumed that anyone driving this sort of car would definitely be a car guy!

But enough pondering – it was time to jump back in and continue the drive…

… down to the end of the road.

This road gets its name from Ashinoko Lake, which comes into view once you reach the very top of the mountain and drive into the observation area. I must have driven past about 10 times that particular day, but I thought it would have been sacrilegious to sacrifice even three minutes of seat time in the McLaren to grab a shot of the lake. Do you blame me?

Being a weekday there were literally no other cars around…

… which made the whole experience even more special.

Right behind this corner is where the other toll gate lies. You are not really supposed to, but most people that come here to ‘drive’ will usually make a U-turn before they reach it.

I’ve experienced Dream Drives in many cars but I couldn’t get enough of the MP4-12C. In fact, only one car has ever had the same effect on me, and that was the Ferrari 458 Spider. These cars are direct rivals and although they deliver very similar performance, it’s done so in two very different ways.

What did I like about the MP4? Well to start off, it was all the details…

… the way the LED taillights and turn signals are integrated into the rear grille…

… and how the optional Inconel exhausts pop out from the center of said grill and provide ridiculously loud and rather badass backfires.

The packaging too, in this drop top especially, I found to be pretty impressive. Again, it’s all a fine fusion between simplicity…

… and presentation, all topped off with McLaren’s signature design. Oh, and that air-brake that automatically tilts when you mash the middle pedal – absolute coolness right there!

You either get it and appreciate it or you don’t. Every detail has its purpose and isn’t there to wow or impress, but because it has a job to serve.

The brakes did well throughout the day, but it was concerning that they gave up in the downhill section that I mentioned previously. If you plan to drive your MP4-12C hard, the carbon ceramic upgrade would definitely be the way to go.

And then we move onto the interior. Swing that beautifully contoured door upward and your’re instantly reminded just how special this car is. Its carbon monocoque chassis is there for everyone to admire, and once you drop down, passed the chunky sill, onto the seat…

… you feel cocooned. I remember our fearless leader Rodbot say the MP4 was a car from the future after his first experience with it, and that is one observation I totally agree with. There’s an almost organic quality to how things have been laid out…

… with the center console the core of the interface.

I love how the LCD navigation screen is oriented vertically – almost like someone had docked their personal iPad mini in there.

The main controls for the driving side of things are within easy reach at the bottom of the center console and are divided between ‘H’ (Handling) and ‘P’ (Powertrain). It takes about 30 minutes of playing around with each combination of settings to figure out what each does and what the best may be for you. I left the Handling in ‘S’ for most of the day and the Powertrain in ‘T’ to get the fastest changes from the dual-clutch transmission. And if you are wondering, yes – the Ferrari 458’s gearbox does shift and react faster. This is definitely an area McLaren can improve. The duality of it all is what really makes the MP4 shine. After you are done driving hard you tone down each setting and let the car transform itself into a useable every day car.

Throw in the fast opening and closing hard top and you have quite the complete package in your hands.

The optional carbon bits fitted to the test car included the seat backs and even the exposed part of the front wheel arch which becomes visible when you open the doors.

After admiring the car in detail it was time to head down to Gotemba city on the opposite side of the mountain chain I was playing on…

… for a spot of lunch. This restaurant happens to be owned by the same company that officially imports McLaren cars into Japan, so once the staff saw me show up they allowed me to park the car on the lawn, safely away from the tight confines of the car park. How nice of them.

Much more thinking about the MP4-12C cabrio was done over some exquisite food – a superb and rewarding way to bring this very special day to an end.

It’s hard not to praise the no-nonsense approach the MP4-12C brings to the supercar experience. It gets the job done and it’s actually a surprisingly easy car to throw around and explore its impressive capabilities. But at the same time I can understand the criticism that it has been subject to from some, saying it could do with a bit more soul and character. To me however, this is just testament that it gets the job done so effectively and without drama. McLaren has definitely managed to sculpt its own identity in a cut-throat market, and the MP4-12C – be it in coupé or open top form – is just the beginning.
