Block And Jr: Hitting Top Gear For Gymkhana

November 2, 2012 Jonathan Moore

Ken Block’s Gymkhana has exploded onto the global scene, taking a form of competition that was previously something more associated with gentle Sunday afternoon auto-testing and throwing in racing technology, a ton of power and the full force of an automotive apocalypse.

This year Ken brought the Gymkhana GRID autocross event across the pond and threw down the gauntlet to European drivers: who could beat him at his own game? Who could win the The European Gauntlet? And it wasn’t just the Gauntlet that he’d be bringing to the UK…

Across four events around Europe, competitors have taken on the Gymkhana course against the clock and their rivals in order to secure a place in the finals.

Starting off in Clermont-Ferrand in France, the Gymkhana qualifying tour then took in Trax at Silverstone, Oschersleben’s Sport1 Trackday in Germany and then the AutoMaxx Super Sunday at Zandvoort in the Netherlands, building up to the finals to be held at the Santa Pod drag strip in the UK.

Block didn’t come alone to the finals: to help out with the hooning he enlisted the help of Formula D buddy Vaughn Gittin Jr.

Vaughn shipped an RTR Mustang over to Europe especially for the event, bringing a loud dose of smoking V8 power to the rear-wheel drive portion of the proceedings.

They would be joined by Liam Doran’s 700hp Citroën DS3 rallycross Supercar…

…which Ken would face off against in his own Fiesta RS rally car in the All-Wheel Drive class.

But before all the hooning for the weekend got underway there was the matter of a little diversion to the west of Santa Pod. Ken and Vaughn had been booked as special guests at Top Gear Live, to be held in the cavernous halls of the Birmingham National Exhibition Centre.

Outside the main entrance, this sign showed that for Top Gear laws are most definitely made to be broken…

Top Gear Live took over three halls, with the main area showing off props from the show and a big collection of exotica from both the road and racing worlds.

Projects used in previous Top Gear Lives filled the intermediary hall, including several examples of Carmageddon: this machine-gun-toting Porsche Carrera…

…and an apocalyptic Capri, looking like Zakspeed had joined forces with Wacky Races and a Panzer division.

With a hint of crossover to where we’d all be on the weekend following TGL, Santa Pod also had a small exhibition area of dragsters: a couple of Funny Cars and then this innocuous looking GTO. Well, innocuous in the fact that you wouldn’t necessarily expect it to have 2,200hp and be able to blast to 60mph in a face-melting 1.3 seconds…

Ken and Vaughn would be putting in guest appearances in their Fiesta rally-car and Mustang drift machine respectively during the two shows in the Live Action Arena on the opening Thursday of the four-day event. Predictably, media attention around the pair was pretty constant.

Both drivers had a single run each of just a couple of minutes duration during each live show, and several dozen cars lined up to take part in each session. That meant there was plenty of time to stroll around the back-stage paddock and take a look at the other metal on show whilst waiting for their turns.

There was a ridiculous number of supercars parked up, which would be taking part in a piece of choreographed mass exotica early in the show. Needless to say these beauties weren’t thrashed like the majority of cars in the arena!

Particularly eye-catching was this ultra-rare Mercedes-Benz CLK GTR Roadster: it’s the only right-hand drive example of just six Roadsters built by M-B around 2006, using the leftovers from the canned Le Mans GT1 programme from 1999.

Homologation rules had meant they nevertheless had to complete the promised 25-car production run, and this is one of the results. These are still fearsome machines, producing well over 600hp from the 6.9-litre turbocharged shallow-angle V12.

Where there’s silver, there’s gold: like this fully bling-tastic, wrapped GT-R.

Next to the pristine supercars were the Top Gear stunt vehicles: mangled wrecks of cars transmogrified and bent to the will of the twisted Top Gear crew for this year’s shows. An army of stunt drivers shuttled between vehicles, taking one set out for their automotive torture session before racing back in to jump into the next batch.

You want a pair of 911s on fire, autocross against snipers or a game of skittles using hatchbacks? Then Top Gear Live is the place to be!

Powering all the mayhem was the backstage special effects crew, who put in a huge effort to keep the spectacle in full flame-throwing, handbrake-pulling top gear. Ahem.

Rallying was due to be celebrated during the live shows, and over half a dozen iconic examples of Subarus, Fords, Mitsubishis and Toyotas were lined up and ready to go.

Vaughn had a good look at some older models from the Ford stable: a mid-90s Escort RS Cosworth and Carlos Sainz’s Focus WRC2000…

…though a Castrol livery combined with a Toyota hits the spot every time.

Of course, the Stig featured heavily in the arena, driving in several demos each session. He could been seen phase-shifting between cars, one minute ‘walking’…

…and the next a car would suddenly appear below him. Strange. As well as taking on a trio of army snipers whilst he autocrossed the new Caterham Superlight R500, he also ran out the new BAC Mono, which is being raved about by reviewers that include our own Bryn Musselwhite.

Every so often Vaughn and anyone else handy would push the car forward a couple of car-lengths as the queue in front gradually shortened with each on-track segment.

Team Japspeed from the British Drift Championship were due to put in a drift demo straight after Vaughn: Shanes Lynch and O’Sullivan would be facing off against each other in the arena, with Shane Lynch already getting in combatitive mood.

So, with a good hour to go until Vaughn’s second run and the Mustang in the safe hands of chief mechanic Dean…

…Vaughn headed into the arena to watch the opening act of the afternoon show.

Nothing about the Top Gear Live show is subtle or grown-up. There’s no attempt by Clarkson, Hammond or May to keep the tone high-brow. After all, this is Top Gear!

The team arrived at full pelt on their V8-powered studio set, before demonstrating that it is possible to donut a stage!

Cameramen lined the side of the arena, all equipped with gas-masks to protect them against the pall of smoke that hung in the air.

Top Gear Live is about jumping and smoking…

…racing and banging.

…slipping and sliding.

…going on fire on four wheels.

…going on fire on two wheels.

…and going on fire on two feet. Generally, the long and short of it is that Top Gear Live is about setting things on fire.

With his slot coming up, Vaughn made his way back to the RTR and started to warm the Mustang up. By far the loudest car on show, the Mustang sounded like a nuclear bomb in the confines of the tented paddock.

Ken Block had just walked past, fresh from finishing his last run of the day, as Vaughn was pushed to the head of the queue.

Then it was time to light it up. There’s an awesome power in the RTR that has to be seen to be believed: it’s looks alive as Vaughn mashes the throttle from a standing start, the Mustang crabbing slowly sideways as it fights the combination of throttle and brake modulation…

Then in a flash he was off and round the corner for his first turn around the packed arena.

Despite the surface being pretty slippery and the entire arena being lined with concrete blocks, Vaughn still hung the rear out all the way round, smoke pouring off the rear tyres.

The crowd loved it of course, though it was over far too quickly and all too soon Vaughn peeled off back into the paddock.

The two Shanes then battled round in their Japspeed Silvias…

…before Shane Lynch was handed a very biased win by co-presenter Vicki Butler-Henderson!

For Vaughn and the crew it was time to pack up and head over to Santa Pod: two days of tyre-smoking fun awaited – which is where we’ll be heading next.

Jonathan Moore
